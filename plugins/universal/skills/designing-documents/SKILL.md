# Designing Documents

Design around what readers need to understand, decide, do, and find. Identify the relationships those tasks require, then make them available through sequence, grouping, explanation, and navigation. Heading changes are one possible result.

## Scope and Contract

Apply at paragraph, section, page, or documentation-set scale, including nontechnical documents. Stay inactive or do very little for spelling fixes, exact translations, quotations, transcripts, single facts, or already effective short passages. Respect fixed forms, requested headings, and generated reference schemas; improve within their constraints. Do not impose an outline, heading depth, audience section, example quota, or publishing system.

Preserve the requested mode:

- **Create:** establish the job and source boundary, draft the document, and report only material assumptions or gaps.
- **Revise:** diagnose actual task failures, preserve meaning and useful structure, and make proportionate edits. Briefly explain consequential changes and unresolved gaps.
- **Review:** do not edit or silently replace the document. Report the reader task, location, problem, effect, and smallest useful remedy. Distinguish predicted failure from observed reader evidence and optional alternatives from defects. No substantive organizational defect is a valid result.

## Working Method

Use these five stages internally. Small tasks need only a brief pass; do not print a worksheet or construct a dependency graph unless it helps the requested work.

### 1. Establish the Job and Boundary

Read the request, relevant material, source authority, and local conventions. Establish mode, intended outcome, likely entry point, and what may change.

Consider six questions: the reader's job, starting capability, subject-specific unfamiliarity, critical relation or misconception, next capability, and entry context. Separate field expertise from familiarity with this subject. An experienced practitioner can still need a product-specific distinction.

Track information as assumed, made available on this path, or not yet established. Presentation does not demonstrate learning. Reserve demonstrated understanding for actual reader evidence; recompute local sufficiency for direct arrivals.

Keep ordinary editorial assumptions internal. State applicability assumptions where readers need them. Ask a precise question when plausible answers change the substantive path, a necessary condition, or source authority. Continue unaffected work; do not invent an unknown guarantee when interaction is unavailable.

### 2. Identify Content and Relationships

Identify necessary claims, definitions, actions, conditions, evidence, examples, and lookup targets. Separate supplied facts, supported inferences, missing facts, and contradictions. For a complex revision, privately track important propositions from source to destination.

Determine which relations matter: prerequisite, part or phase, condition, cause, alternative, evidence or qualification, illustration, or independent target. Connect only supported relations. A connective word cannot establish causation or repair missing evidence. Flag the precise missing proposition; research or substantive completion requires scope that authorizes it.

### 3. Choose Organization and Access Paths

Choose an order for the actual visit:

| Job | Primary order |
| --- | --- |
| Understand | Usable model, necessary relations, interpretable cases |
| Perform | Applicability, prerequisites, actions, branches, verification |
| Decide | Criteria, alternatives, evidence, consequences; recommendation with decisive conditions |
| Troubleshoot | Observable symptoms and checks that distinguish causes |
| Look up | Stable term, error, option, entity, or requirement key |

Mixed jobs can share a document with clear entry points. Keep requirements, rationale, examples, and open questions distinguishable in specifications. Use separate pages when jobs or authority differ materially, not merely because audience labels differ.

Establish a prerequisite before dependent material or briefly beside it. Put a governing condition at the decision or action, before commitment. Keep qualifications attached to their claims. Explain mechanisms and consequences without inventing causation. Compare alternatives using the discriminator. Keep independent targets as peers.

Use a parent only for a meaningful whole; create boundaries where questions, phases, or detail levels change enough to help retrieval. Merge fragments of one task, split competing jobs, reorder dependencies, or leave useful structures alone. Choose descriptive, semantic headings. No fixed section count or nesting depth measures quality.

Keep interdependent task facts together. Optional rationale can move; rare or difficult correctness conditions cannot be hidden as “advanced.” Support direct access without forcing a returning visitor through a lesson. Repeat a short operative condition at independent points of use while keeping one authoritative full account.

### 4. Draft or Restructure

Realize the relationships in finished prose. Use paragraphs for connected reasoning, lists for alternatives or steps, and tables for stable comparisons. A bridge should explain the actual connection; an outline alone may leave it unstated.

Use examples when they expose a needed relation or choice. Supply enough context to interpret the result and avoid a wrong generalization; a brief reference example may need no walkthrough. For circular dependencies, offer a limited provisional model, then refine it. Do not front-load an entire textbook.

Preserve guarantees, exclusions, quantifiers, modality, defaults, units, version scope, authority, provenance, and proposed/adopted status. Do not change code, command order, inputs, outputs, failure handling, or operational behavior incidentally. Technical changes need separate justification and verification. Interface locations and navigation steps are factual claims too; where a result is checked does not establish where its control lives. Preserve substantive information and unrelated work, including necessary repetition. Check affected anchors and known links; local checks cannot establish the absence of external incoming links.

### 5. Check Tasks and Preservation

Walk two paths:

- **First encounter:** can the intended reader interpret the central distinction, follow the relevant relationships, choose or act under the right conditions, and recognize the result?
- **Direct arrival:** at a plausible interior section or page, are subject, applicability, operative conditions, and the route to missing prerequisites or another task locally available?

After moving material, revisit preserved headings as entry points: a working anchor can still strand readers away from a necessary condition or update instruction. Check meaning, examples and code, warnings at use, provenance, links, and requested scope. These are editorial checks, not proof of human comprehension. Stop when intended tasks are supported, material gaps identified, and preservation checks satisfied. Do not expand for every hypothetical reader.

## Composition

Subject-matter review establishes facts; this skill organizes their supported relationships. For technical expression, compose with [writing-technical-prose](../writing-technical-prose/SKILL.md) when relevant. Available prose-style guidance improves expression without removing necessary explanations or useful repetition. Reuse active guidance, scope, and evidence; do not restart workflows or require extra agents. Finish with the same task-and-preservation check.
