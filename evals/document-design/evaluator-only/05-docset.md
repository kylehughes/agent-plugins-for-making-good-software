# Evaluator key: 05-docset

Development. Evaluator only. Test the pages as a set and from direct entry, not only by reading index first.

## Reader tasks and answers

1. **Direct publish entry at `publish.md#publish`.** Ordinary editor saved an article and wants it public. Required answer: Publisher access is required; save/preview is available to ordinary editors but does not publish. Preview shows signed-in editors the saved draft, not public content. With Publisher access, save completed first; check title/date/body in Preview; Publish creates a new numbered public edition; confirm Current in Editions and open permanent address signed out. Conditions belong before or with Publish, including when entering this anchor directly.
2. **Direct restore entry at `restore.md#restore`.** Publisher wants edition 3 public but has an unpublished draft worth keeping. Required: verify article ID and edition number, copy wanted draft text outside Finch before Restore because it replaces draft with no undo/history. Restore copies edition into draft only; public current edition stays visible. Preview/check, then follow publishing procedure to make it public. Destination link must lead to usable instructions, not require returning to index to infer these relations.
3. **Failure during publication.** Publisher has an error and wants to press Publish repeatedly. Required: existing public edition stays unchanged if failure; check Current before retrying an unclear result, keep draft, contact help desk with article ID/error. Do not imply a failed publication erases a public article or Preview link is public proof.
4. **First entry at index.** New editor asks what saving, previewing, publishing, and restoring change. Required: choose correct task page with enough orientation to distinguish draft from published editions. A new article has no editions to restore. No tool-enforced approval workflow exists; external organizational approval is possible but unsupported specifics must not be invented.

## Inference, relationships, and alternatives

Readers know basic editing but not Finch's state model. They may infer that publish after restore creates a new edition if the copy-to-draft and publication relation is explicit. A short overview on index plus self-contained action conditions on task pages is valid. Consolidating explanation on one page with clear local links also works; no fixed information architecture is required. Need not repeat every concept verbatim on every page. Readers must encounter irreversible draft replacement before doing Restore, even on direct entry.

## Preservation and mode

Return three complete pages. Preserve filenames and `publish`, `preview`, and `restore` section anchors, including the supplied cross-links. Existing vague text may be replaced. No action in Finch is authorized. Unauthorized disappearance of pages/anchors is distinct from weak organization. Check links against produced files. Evaluate route length and backtracking, not just presence of keywords.

## High-value evidence

Record the exact path from restore to public result, identity/backup condition placement, whether the Preview/public distinction survives direct entry, and page/link integrity. An excellent landing page cannot compensate for unsafe action sections reached directly.
