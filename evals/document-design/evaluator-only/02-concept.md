# Evaluator key: 02-concept

Development. Evaluator only. Evaluate understanding through the following questions, without giving the reader the source.

## Starting context and tasks

Reader is a team lead who understands requests and permissions but has no knowledge of search indexing. First trial begins at the top of the explanation. Ask: 'I changed a request to Closed and it vanished from search. Is it deleted? What should I do to check?' Correct answer: closing does not delete. Default search omits closed requests; Include closed changes that filter but may still lag. Open a link or Open by number to inspect the current stored record, subject to access. Necessary distinction: a missing search result alone cannot establish deletion, closure, or loss of access.

Second trial starts at whichever heading or paragraph the reader would choose from the question 'Search still shows an item whose page says Request not found.' The evaluator chooses this entry before looking at the arm identity. Ask whether this proves deletion and whether refreshing search will repair it. Correct answer: the search view can retain a deleted record, but Request not found also covers lack of permission. Neither proves deletion. Browser refresh cannot force view catch-up.

Third trial: 'The request page shows the edit, but search still has the old text after three minutes. Is the system breaking a two-minute promise, and what can I send for help?' Correct answer: two minutes is normal behavior, not a guaranteed maximum. Contact service desk with number and observed times after that usual interval; omit private contents. Leads cannot diagnose backlog, force updates, restore deletions, or promise a recovery time.

## Required model and permissible inferences

Stored record and search view must be distinguishable, with change-to-view lag explaining the symptoms. The page is authoritative for permitted current values when loaded, not a permanent snapshot of truth. Another edit may occur. A simple diagram is optional; no implementation or operator procedure is needed. A comparison plus one worked example, narrative explanation, or question-led form may all succeed. Do not demand a fixed heading sequence. Readers may infer 'check the record rather than search for current status' from a clear relationship; they may not infer guaranteed delay or distinguish inaccessible from deleted.

## Mode and preservation

Creation of a short explanation is authorized. All supplied behavior is in scope, but a complete administrator runbook is not. A brief next step is appropriate; invented reindex commands or escalation promises are defects. Score reader understanding independently of whether the producer provided a full finished explanation. Reward restraint when implementation mechanics add no reader value.

## Evidence to retain

Capture the reader's causal account, wrong conclusions, lookup route, and document support for each answer. Merely listing 'search can be delayed' without connecting record, filter, access ambiguity, and the observed symptom is partial credit.
