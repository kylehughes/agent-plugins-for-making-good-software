# Tasks and structure

Use these defaults to resolve an actual organizational decision. They are not mandatory document categories or directories.

## Match the visit

An explanation establishes a usable model and connects its claims. A tutorial additionally needs a controlled task, observable results, and recovery support. A procedure can assume competence in the surrounding activity, but must retain product-specific preconditions and branches. Troubleshooting starts from the symptom the reader can observe, then uses checks to discriminate among causes.

A decision paper names criteria before asking the reader to weigh alternatives. Lead with a recommendation when its decisive conditions can be stated immediately; otherwise identify the unresolved discriminator. A reference preserves its stable retrieval keys, exact contract, defaults, units, and qualifications. Examples and warnings may belong in reference without turning it into a lesson.

Specifications often serve implementers, reviewers, and testers. Separate normative requirements from rationale and examples, and make observable outcomes retrievable. Reorganizing a proposal must not make it appear adopted.

## Relationships and boundaries

Ask what ordinary-language relationship makes children belong to their parent: parts of one mechanism, alternatives within one choice, phases of one task, or evidence for one claim. A shared keyword or a parent named “Details” is not sufficient by itself. Independent questions can remain peer sections, including in a short document.

Merge an action and its separated governing condition. Split a section when it combines independently useful jobs that obscure one another. Reorder when necessary context arrives after its dependent claim. Leave the structure alone when no task benefit is identifiable. Length alone does not justify a section; a short critical branch may deserve one.

Headings identify destinations and express semantic ranks. A heading-outline read checks findability. A headings-hidden read can diagnose disconnected explanatory prose, but is optional: reference entries and labeled tables may legitimately depend on their labels. Judge the complete document.

A transition should identify a real connection: “Previewing saves nothing, so the application can inspect errors before choosing what to persist.” A generic announcement about moving to the next topic supplies no relationship. Keep the supported condition or cause precise; sequence alone is not causal evidence.

## Decide what must remain visible

Classify information as needed to start, needed at a decision/action, needed to interpret the next claim, or optional depth. Only optional depth is a default candidate for an appendix, disclosure, or optional link. A rare condition can govern an ordinary action. Keep it local even when its extended rationale moves elsewhere.

| Common advice | Use when | Adjust when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lead with the answer | The question is defined and the answer can be scoped | A bare answer invites misuse; include its condition or orienting distinction |
| Start with an example | The goal and behavior are immediately interpretable | Copying before learning a condition would be wrong |
| Avoid repetition | It adds no entry-point or task value | An independent point of use needs a short condition |
| Put advanced details later | They are optional for this visit | They determine whether the action is correct |
| Lead with prerequisites | They are needed to begin | They concern later branches; supply them locally |
| Organize by taxonomy | Visitors search by entity | Visitors must choose or act across entities; expose criteria/workflow too |

## Examples that teach a relationship

Choose an example for the relation or decision it makes visible. A new conceptual model may need an ordinary case and one contrasting case; a reference may need one line. Explain the important result and any boundary needed to prevent overgeneralization. Additional examples should do different work, not merely increase volume.

For circular dependencies, introduce a small provisional model with explicit limits. Refine it when the reader can interpret the next distinction. This avoids recursively teaching all background before the task starts.

A nearby changed-condition question can expose whether an explanation supports application beyond copying. An editorial walkthrough predicts usefulness; only actual reader evidence demonstrates understanding.
